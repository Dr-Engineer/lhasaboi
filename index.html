Ringtone Maker

Turn any part of a song into an iPhone ringtone or alarm sound.

Free. Your song never leaves this device: nothing is uploaded.

1 Choose a song

Any audio file you have: MP3, M4A, WAV, AAC, FLAC… You can also drop it on this page. Songs saved inside Apple Music, Spotify or YouTube Music won't work, because they're copy-protected.

2 Pick the part

Start
Fades & volume

Tap the waveform to jump, drag to move the highlighted part. Keys: Space plays, ← → nudge (hold Shift for 1 s). iPhone ringtones must be under 30 seconds.

3 Make the ringtone

Format

Download

  1. Tap Share… and choose Use as Ringtone (scroll the list if you don't see it).
    Not listed? Choose Save to Files. Then in the Files app, touch and hold the file, tap Share, then Use as Ringtone.
  2. Tap Download. Then open the Files app → Downloads, touch and hold the file, tap Share, then Use as Ringtone.
  3. Pick it in Settings → Sounds & Haptics → Ringtone. For an alarm: Clock → Alarms → tap the alarm → Sound.
  1. Download the file.
  2. Send it to your iPhone: email or message it to yourself, or save it to iCloud Drive, OneDrive or Google Drive.
  3. On the iPhone, open the file (in Mail, Messages or the Files app), tap Share, then Use as Ringtone.
  4. Pick it in Settings → Sounds & Haptics → Ringtone, or in Clock as an alarm sound.

“Use as Ringtone” needs iOS 26 or later. On an older iOS, see the help below.

Use it as an alarm

Open Clock → Alarms, tap an alarm (or + for a new one), then Sound. Your ringtones are listed under Ringtones. Each alarm has its own sound, so set it on each alarm you want.

No “Use as Ringtone” option?

It needs iOS 26 or later (check in Settings → General → About). iPhone 11 and newer can update. If you're on iOS 26 and it still isn't there, update to the latest iOS 26 version and restart the phone. If the iPhone won't take the MP3, make it again as WAV.

Older iPhone (iOS 18 or earlier): use GarageBand
  1. Make the ringtone here and tap Save to Files.
  2. Install GarageBand (free, by Apple). Start a new song with Audio Recorder → Voice.
  3. Switch to the tracks view (the button that looks like stacked bricks), tap the loop icon at the top right, then Files → Browse items from the Files app, and pick your ringtone.
  4. Drag it onto the track, all the way to the left. Tap + at the right end of the timeline → Section A → turn on Automatic, so it isn't cut short.
  5. Go back to My Songs, touch and hold the project, then Share → Ringtone → Export.
Getting songs from a Windows PC

Easiest: open this page on the PC, make the ringtone there, then send the small file to your phone (email or message it to yourself) and open it on the iPhone.

Or: put song files in iCloud Drive (iCloud for Windows), OneDrive or Google Drive on the PC. They appear in the iPhone's Files app, so you can pick them right here on the phone.

Put this on your Home Screen

In Safari, tap Share → Add to Home Screen. It then opens like an app.

Everything happens in your browser; your songs are never uploaded. Ringtones you add stay on your iPhone even if you delete the file. MP3 encoding by LAME via lamejs (LGPL). Source
